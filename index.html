Exploring quantum computing for drug discovery

QuantumMed is a physics project that estimates the ground-state energy of a molecule with the Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE), starting with water (H₂O).

View the water moleculeRead the methodology

Workflow

  1. Molecular modelling

    Define the molecule, geometry, charge, spin and basis set.

  2. Hamiltonian

    Build the electronic Hamiltonian and map it to qubit operators.

  3. VQE

    Prepare a parameterised quantum state and minimise its energy with a classical optimiser.

  4. Data analysis

    Inspect convergence and compare with reference values.

  5. Results

    Report the energies found and their limitations.

Analysis

Methodology and limitations

Workflow

A molecule's geometry and basis set define its electronic Hamiltonian. A fermion-to-qubit mapping (for example Jordan–Wigner) turns it into a weighted sum of Pauli operators that a quantum circuit can measure.

VQE

A parameterised circuit (the ansatz), often starting from the Hartree–Fock state, prepares a trial state. The energy expectation value is measured and a classical optimiser updates the parameters to lower it. By the variational principle, the result is an upper bound on the true ground-state energy.

Total energy

The qubit Hamiltonian covers the electronic part only. The nuclear repulsion energy is added separately to get the total molecular energy.

Limitations

  • Simulations on classical hardware are not runs on a physical quantum computer, and noise-free simulation does not show real-device performance.
  • Small basis sets such as STO-3G limit accuracy compared with experiment and larger-basis methods.
  • A finite number of optimiser evaluations does not by itself prove convergence; the optimiser and starting point can trap the result in a local minimum.
  • Water is a small test case. Drug-sized molecules need far more qubits and circuit depth than today's methods and devices comfortably handle.
  • This project does not claim a quantum advantage.
